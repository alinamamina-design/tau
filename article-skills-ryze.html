The six core skills, with setup guides

These six do most of the lifting, one per stage. Each guide lists what the skill reads, what it ships and when to use it, followed by the full prompt. Paste it into Claude as a skill or a project instruction, and it is live.

Career Audit: The honest baseline: which roles you’re strongest for

Most people apply to the title they have, not the role they would win. The audit reads your CV and past work, scores you against five to eight target roles and ranks them by evidence, not by hope.

  • Reads: Your CV, your LinkedIn profile and 3–5 roles you’re considering
  • Ships: A ranked list of target roles with the evidence for each
  • When: Once at the start, again after every 20 applications. Targets drift.
Skill prompt, paste into Claude
You are my Career Auditor.
Job: tell me which roles I'm strongest for, with evidence.
Process:
1. Read my CV and LinkedIn profile. List my skills, scope and results.
2. Score me 1–5 against each target role: skills, seniority, domain, results.
3. Rank the roles. For each, quote the 2 strongest lines of evidence from my CV.
4. Name the one gap that costs me the most interviews.
Output: a ranked table of role, evidence, gap. No praise, no filler.

Role Scan: New roles within the hour, ranked by fit and pay

The first fifty applicants get read; the next five hundred get filtered. Role Scan checks LinkedIn on a schedule, drops anything below your level or pay floor and hands you a short list while the posting is still fresh.

  • Reads: LinkedIn jobs for your titles, locations and pay floor
  • Ships: A ranked shortlist of roles posted in the last hour
  • When: Every hour on workdays. A role is freshest in its first 24 hours.
Skill prompt, paste into Claude
You are my Role Scanner.
Job: find new roles that fit me, before everyone else.
Process:
1. Search roles posted in the last 60 minutes for my target titles.
2. DROP: below my pay floor, wrong level, wrong location, agencies.
3. KEEP: score fit 1–5 on title, level, stack and pay.
4. Flag roles with fewer than 25 applicants.
Output: a table of role, company, salary, applicants, fit. Best first.

CV Tailor: Your CV rewritten around the posting, without inventing anything

An ATS reads keywords; a recruiter reads the top third in six seconds. CV Tailor maps the posting’s language onto your real experience, reorders bullets by relevance and returns a clean one-page PDF.

  • Reads: Your master CV and the job posting
  • Ships: A tailored one-page CV and a list of what changed
  • When: For every application. A generic CV is a filtered CV.
Skill prompt, paste into Claude
You are my CV Tailor.
Job: make my CV fit this posting using only what's true.
Process:
1. Extract the 10 keywords and 3 must-haves from the posting.
2. Match each to evidence in my CV. Mark gaps; never invent.
3. Rewrite the summary and top 6 bullets in the posting's language, with numbers.
4. One page. ATS-safe: no tables, no columns, no icons.
Output: the tailored CV, then a change log with keyword coverage.

Outreach Message: A three-sentence note the hiring manager actually answers

Applications sit in a queue; messages land in an inbox. Outreach Message finds one specific hook in the person’s team or recent posts and writes a note short enough to read on a phone.

  • Reads: The role, the hiring manager’s profile and their recent posts
  • Ships: A note under 300 characters and a day-5 follow-up
  • When: Within an hour of applying, while your application is on top.
Skill prompt, paste into Claude
You are my Outreach Writer.
Job: get a reply from the person who owns this role.
Process:
1. Read their profile and last 5 posts. Find one specific hook.
2. Write 3 sentences: the hook, my most relevant result, one clear ask.
3. Under 300 characters. No "I hope you're well", no CV summary.
4. Write a 2-line follow-up for day 5.
Output: the note, the follow-up, and the hook you used.

Interview Prep: A one-page brief for Thursday’s interview

Most interview prep is rereading the job post. Interview Prep researches the company, the team and the people on the panel, predicts the questions and maps each one to a story you already have.

  • Reads: The posting, the company site, the interviewers’ profiles, your story bank
  • Ships: A one-page brief: likely questions, matched stories, your questions
  • When: Two days before each interview. Enough time to rehearse, not to forget.
Skill prompt, paste into Claude
You are my Interview Coach.
Job: prepare me for this interview in 20 minutes of reading.
Process:
1. Summarise the company: product, business model, last 3 news items.
2. Profile each interviewer: role, focus, what they'll test.
3. List the 10 most likely questions. Match each to one of my stories.
4. Write 3 sharp questions for me to ask them.
Output: a one-page brief. Questions first, stories second.

Negotiation Script: The counter-offer, scripted for the call and the email

The first offer is a starting point, and the people who ask usually get some of what they ask for. Negotiation Script anchors on market data, writes the counter and prepares a fallback for every likely answer.

  • Reads: The offer, your target number and market salary data
  • Ships: A call script, a counter email and a fallback ladder
  • When: Within 48 hours of an offer, before you say yes.
Skill prompt, paste into Claude
You are my Negotiation Coach.
Job: get me the best offer without risking it.
Process:
1. Compare the offer to market data for this role, level and city.
2. Set the counter: my target plus 10%, justified by 2 facts.
3. Script the call: thanks, enthusiasm, the number, silence.
4. Prepare fallbacks: signing bonus, equity, start date, title.
Output: call script, counter email, fallback ladder.

What makes a skill more than a prompt

A prompt is a request. A skill is a role with a method. It has a written job, so the output is the same kind of thing every time; a real input, your CV and the posting, not a summary from memory; a fixed output, a table, a PDF or a script you can act on; and a moment, the point in the week when it runs. Take any of those away and you are back to re-explaining the task in a new chat.

Start with one stage

Don’t add all eighteen on day one. Most searches stall because the target is too wide, not because the CV is weak, so start with Career Audit and Role Scan. Add CV Tailor once you have a short list worth applying to. When running the loop by hand starts eating your evenings, hand it to Tau.

Frequently asked questions

What are Claude skills for job search?

A Claude skill is a reusable instruction file that teaches Claude one method and the standard its output has to meet. A job search skill encodes one piece of the craft — tailoring a CV, scoring a role, writing outreach — so it runs the same way every time instead of being re-explained in a new prompt.

How many skills are in this pack?

Eighteen, across six stages: positioning, search, applications, outreach, interviews and offers. They’re built to be used one at a time, and each names the output it produces.

Do I need a paid Claude plan?

Skills work on Claude’s paid and free plans where skills are available. Each skill is plain Markdown, so you can also paste one into a chat as an instruction — you lose the automatic pick-up but keep the method.

How do I install a skill?

Unzip the pack and add each SKILL.md in Claude under Settings → Skills. Claude then uses the skill on its own whenever your request matches what it’s for.

Which skill should I start with?

Career Audit, then Fit Score. A sharp target and a short list of roles worth your time beat a perfect CV sent to the wrong jobs.

Can the skills apply for me?

No — the skills draft; you send. If you want the whole loop to run every day — finding roles, tailoring, applying and booking interviews — launch the Tau agent on LinkedIn.
